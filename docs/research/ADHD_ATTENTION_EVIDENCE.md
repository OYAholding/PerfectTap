# Can a tap-timing game support attention practice? An honest evidence review for PerfectTap

**Prepared:** 2026-11-21 · **Status:** research document, not clinical advice. Hebrew summary: [ADHD_ATTENTION_EVIDENCE.he.md](ADHD_ATTENTION_EVIDENCE.he.md).
**Related:** [MODE_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md](MODE_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md) · [COMPLIANCE.md](COMPLIANCE.md) · [USER_STUDY_PLAN.md](USER_STUDY_PLAN.md)

## How to read this

**Citations.** Every claim links to its source.
- DOIs and PubMed IDs were checked against PubMed's E-utilities records.
- Unless noted, numbers come from abstracts.
- Items marked **[unverified]** could not be confirmed and should not be quoted externally without checking.

**Outcome classes.** Results are always sorted into three classes, because confusing them is the most common way brain-training evidence is overstated:

| Class | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Practiced task** | Getting better at the game itself | Higher PerfectTap accuracy after a week of play |
| **(b) Transfer** | *Near*: a similar untrained task. *Far*: a different cognitive task. | (b-near) a different tapping task; (b-far) a CPT, stop-signal or working-memory test |
| **(c) Real world** | Everyday concentration, ADHD symptoms, school or work functioning | Parent/teacher ADHD rating scales, impairment ratings |

**Rater blinding** matters as much as the outcome class:
- **Unblinded (MPROX).** "Most proximal" raters, usually parents who know the child is being trained.
- **Probably blinded (PBLIND).** Teachers or observers unaware of allocation, and objective tests.

Across the ADHD literature, effects shrink sharply when only blinded ratings are counted.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **PerfectTap will almost certainly improve players at PerfectTap (a).** Practice reliably improves the practiced task. This is uncontroversial ([Simons et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100616661983)).
2. **Transfer to other attention tasks (b) is unlikely and unproven.**
   - Meta-analyses of computerized cognitive training (CCT) in ADHD find near transfer mainly for working-memory training to other working-memory tasks.
   - They find essentially **no transfer to untrained attention or inhibition tasks** ([Westwood et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-023-02000-7); [Rapport et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2013.08.005)).
   - Rapport et al. found that programs **specifically training attention did not improve attention**.
3. **Effects on everyday concentration or ADHD symptoms (c) are small to null when raters are blinded.**
   - Pooled effects of SMD 0.37–0.48 from unblinded parents fall to 0.12–0.20 with blinded raters ([Cortese et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2014.12.010); [Westwood et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-023-02000-7)).
   - The largest blinded meta-analysis found only a small, short-term, setting-specific effect on inattention (SMD 0.17).
4. **The one FDA-authorized ADHD game is the strongest case, and it is still modest.**
   - EndeavorRx improved a computerized attention test (TOVA) in a double-blind RCT.
   - It did **not** show statistically significant between-group differences on parent or clinician symptom scales.
   - Its FDA label says patients "may not display benefits in typical behavioral symptoms" ([FDA DEN200026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN200026.pdf)).
5. **Timing is a relevant target behavior, not a proven training route.**
   - People with ADHD show, on average, **more variable sensorimotor timing and reaction times** ([Noreika et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2012.09.036); [Kofler et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2013.06.001)). PerfectTap therefore exercises and can *measure* a behavior on which ADHD groups differ.
   - Variability is **not specific** to ADHD, and **non-drug interventions have not been shown to reduce it** ([Kofler et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2013.06.001)).
   - Rhythm/timing training evidence for ADHD rests on one small, conflicted 2001 trial and recent pilots.
6. **Honest positioning:**
   - Supported: "a timing game designed with ADHD players in mind, which shows you your own timing consistency".
   - Not supported: "trains attention", "improves focus" or "helps ADHD". Making these claims would also be a regulatory problem ([COMPLIANCE.md](COMPLIANCE.md)).
   - Whether PerfectTap practice transfers is an **open, testable question** ([USER_STUDY_PLAN.md](USER_STUDY_PLAN.md)).

## 2. Cognitive constructs: what ADHD groups differ on, and whether PerfectTap engages it

This section describes **group differences** (ADHD vs comparison), not training effects. A group difference makes a construct relevant to measure. It does not imply that practicing it helps.

### 2.1 Sustained attention (vigilance)
- **Measurement:** usually measured with continuous performance tests (CPTs) such as TOVA or Conners CPT. These present a long, *externally paced* stream of stimuli. The player must respond to targets and withhold to non-targets.
- **ADHD difference:**
  - A meta-analysis of 47 CPT studies in children found **large** overall ADHD–control differences, driven by lower perceptual sensitivity and slower evidence accumulation (drift rate).
  - It found only **small-to-moderate** evidence for a *decline over time* (vigilance decrement) ([Huang-Pollock et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0027205)).
- **PerfectTap:** the core loop is self-paced. Nothing is missed if the player looks away, and a centre crossing that passes untapped has no cost. So the core game **does not require vigilance in the CPT sense**. Timed and Rush modes add time pressure, but still no omission penalty. → See the non-self-paced protocol proposal in the matrix.

### 2.2 Selective attention
- **Definition:** attending to relevant stimuli while ignoring distractors.
- **PerfectTap:** there is little to select between. There is one moving object, and visual effects (particles, popups, combo text) act as incidental distractors rather than structured distractors. **Invisible Zone** and **Mirror** add some demand on tracking the centre or the reversals. None of this is a validated selective-attention manipulation.

### 2.3 Response inhibition
- **Measurement:** stop-signal and go/no-go tasks.
- **ADHD difference:**
  - ADHD groups show longer stop-signal reaction times (SSRT) ([Lijffijt et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-843X.114.2.216)).
  - A meta-analysis suggests these SSRT differences may reflect **general attention or processing inefficiency** more than inhibition per se ([Alderson et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-007-9131-6)).
- **PerfectTap:** players must *wait* until the indicator approaches centre, because premature taps score as misses. That is a form of response timing / restraint. It is **not** the cancellation of a prepotent response to an unpredictable signal, which is what stop-signal and go/no-go tasks measure. A go/no-go variant would be needed (matrix §4).
- **Gap:** no go/no-go meta-analysis was verified for this document.

### 2.4 Reaction time
- **What the evidence shows:** in ADHD, the robust finding is **variability**, not slowness. After accounting for reaction-time variability (RTV), mean RT is not slower ([Kofler et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2013.06.001)).
- **PerfectTap:** the game does not measure simple reaction time. The indicator's path is predictable, so taps are **anticipatory** (coincidence-anticipation timing). Only Mirror, Chaos and the speed shocks in Hardcore Combo introduce unpredictable events that require reactive correction.

### 2.5 Timing precision
- **ADHD difference:**
  - A systematic review found consistent ADHD impairments in motor timing, perceptual timing and temporal foresight. **Sensorimotor synchronization** (tapping in time) was among the most consistent findings ([Noreika, Falter & Rubia 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2012.09.036); this is a review, not a meta-analysis).
  - Supporting studies:
    - Greater synchronization variability in ADHD boys, which methylphenidate reduced ([Rubia et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1023233630774)).
    - Less consistent timing on Wing–Kristofferson analysis ([Zelaznik et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.humov.2011.05.003)).
    - Poorer beat tracking in children and adults ([Puyjarinet et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-11295-w)).
  - Directly relevant to game design, adults with ADHD had **higher tapping variability**, but **intact low-level phase correction**. The authors suggest the variability may stem partly from higher-level factors such as sustained attention ([Hove et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00221-017-5089-y)).
- **PerfectTap:** timing precision is **what PerfectTap most directly exercises**. Tap error relative to centre, and its consistency over a session, is a sensible in-game *measure*. It is not a diagnostic and it is not validated against clinical outcomes.
- **Gap:** no meta-analysis of sensorimotor synchronization variability in ADHD was located.

### 2.6 Working memory
- **ADHD difference:** large differences, especially spatial ([Martinussen et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.chi.0000153228.72591.73); [Kasper et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2012.07.001)).
- **PerfectTap:** the game places **minimal working-memory load**. Working memory is the domain with the most near-transfer evidence, so PerfectTap is unlikely to inherit even that.

### 2.7 Attention regulation / state regulation
- **Theory:**
  - The **cognitive-energetic (state-regulation) model** proposes that ADHD performance depends on arousal and effort allocation. Performance is therefore sensitive to **event rate**: very slow or very fast pacing can worsen it ([Sergeant 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2004.09.010)).
  - The **default-mode interference** hypothesis proposes periodic intrusions at periods of about 10 s or more, producing lapses ([Sonuga-Barke & Castellanos 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2007.02.005)).
  - Evidence that motivation and event rate modulate RTV is **mixed** ([Tamm et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13311-012-0138-5)).
- **PerfectTap:**
  - Self-pacing lets players regulate their own event rate. That may aid comfort and engagement, but it makes sessions less comparable.
  - Lapse-like patterns would only appear in sessions long enough to observe them (minutes, not seconds).

### 2.8 Performance consistency (RT variability / intra-individual variability)
- **ADHD association:** a meta-analysis of 319 studies found greater RTV in ADHD than typically developing peers: Hedges' g ≈ 0.76 in children/adolescents and 0.46 in adults ([Kofler et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2013.06.001)).
- **Not specific to ADHD:**
  - Adolescents and adults with ADHD were indistinguishable from *clinical* control groups.
  - Children were only slightly more variable than clinical controls (g ≈ 0.25).
  - Karalunas et al. call RTV a proposed **trans-diagnostic** phenotype, decomposable into ex-Gaussian *tau* and drift rate ([Karalunas et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12217); see also [Tamm et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13311-012-0138-5)).
  - RTV was proposed early as a candidate endophenotype ([Castellanos & Tannock 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn896)).
- **Response to treatment:** stimulants reduced RTV (g ≈ −0.74). **Psychosocial and non-stimulant interventions had no effect** ([Kofler et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2013.06.001)).
- **Implication for PerfectTap:**
  - A "consistency" metric (SD of signed tap error, slow-tail/lapse rate) aligns with a well-replicated ADHD-associated feature, so it is a defensible thing to *show players*.
  - It cannot be presented as an ADHD indicator, a diagnosis or an attention score.
  - Improvement in it is class (a) unless shown otherwise.

**Working memory training more broadly** (mixed populations, not only ADHD):
- Short-lived near transfer, and "no convincing evidence" of far transfer ([Melby-Lervåg & Hulme 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028228)).
- No reliable far transfer versus active controls, and working-memory gains did not predict far-transfer gains ([Melby-Lervåg, Redick & Hulme 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616635612)).

**Individual trials worth knowing:**
- **Cogmed:**
  - The early positive RCT used a non-adaptive comparison, with symptom gains rated by parents ([Klingberg et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1097/00004583-200502000-00010)).
  - A well-matched placebo replication found working-memory *storage* gains, but **no** differences on parent/teacher ratings, objective attention or academics. Its authors concluded Cogmed "should not be considered a viable treatment" for ADHD ([Chacko et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12146)).
- **Attention-specific training ("Pay Attention!"):** parent and clinician ratings improved against a waitlist, but teacher ratings did not, and only one of the neuropsychological measures improved ([Tamm et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2012.11.004)). This is the classic proximal-versus-blinded split.
- **Braingame Brian:** a double-blind, gamified, three-arm RCT. All arms, including placebo, improved on symptom ratings with **no condition × time interaction**. The authors attribute the symptom change to "mainly nonspecific treatment factors" ([Dovis et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0121651)).

**Reading across the evidence:**
- (a) is robust.
- (b-near) exists for working memory.
- (b-far) to attention and inhibition is essentially null.
- (c) shrinks toward zero with blinding.
- Gamified placebos produce symptom "improvement" too. This is the main reason any PerfectTap study needs an active control and blinded outcomes.

## 4. Broader brain-training critique

- **Simons et al. 2016**, "Do 'Brain-Training' Programs Work?", *Psychol Sci Public Interest* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100616661983), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27697851/)):
  - Reviewed every study cited by brain-training companies.
  - Found "extensive evidence" for (a), less for closely related tasks, little for distant tasks, and little for everyday cognition (c).
  - **No study met all best-practice criteria**: active control, preregistration, blinding, expectation measurement. These criteria are used as the checklist in [USER_STUDY_PLAN.md](USER_STUDY_PLAN.md).
- **Sala, Tatlidil & Gobet 2018**, *Psychol Bull* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000139)): three meta-analyses, k ≈ 300+ each. Video-game training does not enhance general cognitive ability; small or null effects.
- **Gobet & Sala 2023**, *Perspect Psychol Sci* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916221091830)): the far-transfer effect across training types is essentially null, with little true heterogeneity.
- **Music training:** [Sala & Gobet 2020](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-020-01060-2) is relevant to rhythm claims (title and series indicate no robust far transfer; abstract not reviewed).
- **FTC v. Lumos Labs (2016):** a $2M settlement ($50M judgment suspended). The challenged claims included reducing cognitive impairment associated with **ADHD** ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges-its-brain-training-program)). This is the direct regulatory precedent for game-based ADHD claims.

## 5. EndeavorRx (AKL-T01): what was actually shown

### 5.1 STARS-ADHD pivotal RCT
**Source:** [Kollins et al. 2020, *Lancet Digital Health*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(20)30017-0) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33334505/); NCT02674633)

**Design:**
- Randomized, double-blind, 20 sites.
- **348 unmedicated children aged 8–12.**
- Children had to have ADHD-RS ≥ 28 **and** a TOVA-demonstrated attention deficit (API ≤ −1.8).
- Dose: 25 min/day, 5 days/week, for 4 weeks.
- Control: a digital word game matched for engagement and reward, without the adaptive engine.

**Outcomes by class:**

| Outcome | Class | Result |
|---|---|---|
| TOVA Attention Performance Index (primary) | (b), a CPT different from the trained game | Change +0.93 vs +0.03, p = 0.006. Median difference 0.88 (95% CI 0.24–1.49). Roughly **0.28 SD** (our back-of-envelope calculation, not reported by the authors). |
| ADHD-RS, IRS, CGI-I, BRIEF (parent/clinician; secondary) | (c) | **No statistically meaningful difference** across the 7 secondary parent/clinician scales, per the FDA decision summary. Numerical trends favored AKL-T01; IRS showed a trend **[exact p unverified]**. Both arms improved on symptom scales. |
| Adverse events | — | Frustration ~3%, headache ~2%, no serious events |

Adherence was 83 of 100 expected sessions. The study was sponsored by Akili.

### 5.2 Follow-on studies (uncontrolled)
- **STARS-Adjunct** ([Kollins et al. 2021, *npj Digit Med*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-021-00429-0)): **open-label, single-arm**. IRS improved in medicated and unmedicated cohorts (c, unblinded). With no control, expectancy and regression to the mean cannot be excluded.
- **Adolescents and adults** ([Stamatis et al. 2024, *npj Ment Health Res*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44184-024-00075-w)): **single-arm**. TOVA improved; the authors call the results "preliminary". TOVA practice effects are a major confound.

### 5.3 FDA De Novo DEN200026 (granted 2020-06-15)
**Source:** the [decision summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN200026.pdf). It created the device type "digital therapy device for ADHD" (21 CFR 882.5803, Class II, **prescription only**).

**Indication:**
- To improve "attention function as measured by computer-based testing" in children 8–12 with inattentive or combined-type ADHD who have a demonstrated attention issue.
- Patients "may not display benefits in typical behavioral symptoms, such as hyperactivity."
- It is to be used as part of a broader therapeutic program, **not as a stand-alone therapy**.

**Later history:**
- **Label expansion:** the label was extended to ages 13–17 in **December 2023**, on the basis of the single-arm adolescent study (trade press and company release; not re-verified on an FDA page). This was not in 2024.
- **Corporate fate:** Akili was acquired by Virtual Therapeutics in July 2024 at $0.434/share (tender offer, SEC filings via search). FDA authorization did not by itself produce commercial success.

**What this means for PerfectTap:**
- Even a purpose-built, adaptive, multitask game, tested in a large double-blind RCT, demonstrated **(b) transfer to a CPT**, but **not (c) blinded symptom change**.
- PerfectTap is single-mechanic and self-paced, and has no evidence. It should not imply any comparable benefit.

| Brain Balance + IM: [Teicher et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2022.114973) | Open study, 16 completers, 59% dropout | Parent ratings improved (unblinded) | **No** change on the objective attention measure. Authors: an RCT is required. |
| Rhythm Workers: [Jamey et al. 2025, *Behav Res Methods*](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-025-02802-3) | Feasibility RCT, 27 children 7–13. Beat-synchronization tapping game vs matched active control | Rhythmic abilities improved (a/b-near). Executive-function gains reported within-group only | n = 27. Within-group inference. **The closest existing analogue to PerfectTap.** |
| [Zhu 2026, *Front Public Health*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1808386) | RCT n = 64, rhythm game vs routine health education | Stroop, TMT-B, motor and ADHD ratings all improved | Passive control. Blinding not reported. Implausibly uniform effects. Treat with caution. |

**Verdict:** the rationale is reasonable, since timing is impaired on average in ADHD (§2.5). But the training evidence is **weak**: small samples, unblinded or passive controls, multiplicity, and developer involvement. No study has tested a **self-paced, single-mechanic tap-timing game** with blinded outcomes.

## 7. Serious games and gamification

**Reviews of video games for ADHD:**
- [Peñuelas-Calvo et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-020-01557-w) is a systematic review (**not** a meta-analysis) of 22 studies. Assessment games discriminated ADHD from controls. Intervention games were "well accepted and generally effective", but the synthesis is qualitative, with no pooled effects or blinding analysis, so read it optimistically at best.
- [Rodrigo-Yanguas et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.798480) is a quasi-systematic review of 128 papers of mixed designs. It also covers **problematic video-game use** in ADHD.
- [Lau et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2016.00209) found g = 0.55 for serious games in mental health, but against no-intervention controls and with only one ADHD study.

**Gamification and motivation:**
- **Reinforcement:** it improves performance in both ADHD and control children, somewhat more in ADHD. Children with ADHD prefer immediate rewards ([Luman et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2004.11.001)). This is consistent with the delay-aversion pathway of the dual-pathway model ([Sonuga-Barke 2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2003.08.005)).
- **Game format:** game-format training increased time-on-task and training performance in children with ADHD ([Prins et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2009.0206)).
- **Assessment caveat:** game elements boost engagement, but they can change *what a task measures* ([Lumsden et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.2196/games.5888)).

**The key lesson** comes from Braingame Brian ([Dovis et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0121651)) and the EndeavorRx control arm. Gamification drives **engagement and adherence**, while symptom ratings improve **regardless of the active ingredient**. Engagement is valuable for a game. It is not evidence of benefit.

**Problematic use:**
- **Observational evidence:** in a cohort of 2,587 adolescents, each additional high-frequency digital-media activity was associated with slightly higher odds of later ADHD symptoms, OR 1.10 ([Ra et al. 2018, *JAMA*](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.8931)). The effect is modest and causality is not established.
- **Design argument:** this supports avoiding compulsive-loop mechanics. Examples include streak punishment, loss-framed timers and variable-ratio paid rewards.

**Overstimulation:** no controlled evidence was found that particles, sound or screen effects help or harm attention performance in ADHD (**gap**). Calm-presentation options are therefore a **comfort and accessibility** decision, not an evidence-based efficacy feature.

## 8. Adaptive difficulty

- **The direct test:** [von Bastian & Eschen 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-015-0655-z) randomized 130 young adults (not ADHD) to adaptive, randomized-difficulty, self-selected-difficulty or active-control conditions.
  - Training procedure did **not** change training gains or transfer. Exposure to varying difficulty was sufficient.
  - The usual "adaptive vs easiest level" comparison confounds adaptivity with exposure to difficulty.
- **ADHD trials:** in Cogmed and AKL-T01, adaptivity was confounded with other differences between arms.
- **Conclusion:** an adaptive staircase is **good game design**, because it keeps challenge near the player's level and can limit frustration. It is also **useful for measurement**: a staircase threshold is a compact performance index. But **adaptivity is not, by itself, evidence of transfer**.
